Q3 2026 Earnings Release

 

A Note to Our

Shareholders

Dear Shareholders,

Our third quarter results were in line with expectations, with non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA improving by $4.5 million year over year and non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA margin expanding by over 1,000 basis points. We also made important progress on our product transformation, rolling out a redesigned, messaging-first student experience and advancing new self-service e-commerce capabilities. Together, these improvements are designed to make it easier for families to get started, deliver more value during and in-between tutoring sessions, and create a more efficient, scalable business.

The third quarter marked an important transition in Nerdy’s evolution. Throughout 2026, we have been building a new Consumer learning experience, simplifying our business, and applying AI to improve our products and operations. In recent weeks, major parts of that work have moved into customers’ hands. That includes a large portion of our reimagined new student experience, with the remaining features and products being launched in the coming weeks.

We have improved our self-service and e-commerce capabilities and now can support a registration-led path to membership. Our acquisition model has historically centered on generating leads and converting them through sales. The new experience enables customers to register, purchase, and begin their learning membership through a self-service checkout flow, without requiring a sales conversation. Over time, we expect this more modern way to transact with our customers to lower customer acquisition costs, expand reach, and support a more efficient and scalable go-to-market model.

These capabilities are arriving later than planned, limiting their contribution during the back-to-school acquisition window. We acquired fewer new learning membership customers than anticipated and entered the fourth quarter with a smaller recurring revenue base. Our Q4 revenue outlook reflects that shortfall in learning membership counts.

The new student experience changes how families use Nerdy. Instead of navigating menus and pages, learners and parents simply tell us what they need, and the platform does it. More than 100 agentic actions sit behind that conversation. A student can get step-by-step help on tonight’s homework, turn next week’s test into a personalized study plan with reminders, generate practice on exactly what they’re struggling with, schedule or reschedule a session, message their tutor, or enroll in a live class, all in the same thread. Everything in a learning membership, from 1-on-1 tutoring to live classes to AI practice, is now one message away. We believe this makes the learning membership more valuable between sessions, and increasing engagement between tutoring sessions is one of the most direct ways we can elevate retention and customer lifetime value.

We introduced a new capability called Smart Actions in the quarter that brings real-time AI directly into live instruction. It acts as a real-time assistant alongside the human tutor, displaying superimposed suggestions directly over the digital classroom screen. Tutors can utilize suggested 3D interactive models, visual explanations, and lesson content into the shared lesson with a click, pulling from the more than 15,000 prepared lessons we now have that span some 200 subjects. A tutor explaining circulation, for example, can place a three-dimensional heart on screen and show the student how blood moves through it, which is both a more effective way to teach and also far more engaging and stimulating.

For our youngest learners, we built an entirely separate elementary experience. It is rocket and space themed, visually rich, and designed from the ground up for K-5 students rather than a universal dashboard and experience for all

Third quarter revenue was $33.2 million, within guidance. GAAP net loss was $12.8 million. Non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA loss was $5.7 million, which was better than our guidance range and represented a $4.5 million improvement from a year ago. Our total headcount was down 44% at the end of Q3 versus last year. Excluding the Varsity Tutors for Schools exit, our Consumer sales, support, engineering, and product teams are leaner by more than 300 people, as AI now handles work that previously required additional headcount. After seeing a rapid spike in AI spend in the spring, by September our annualized AI spending rate was approximately 60% below its peak during June and July due to improved model selection strategies and other tactics while internal AI adoption continued to accelerate. We expect fourth quarter revenue of $37 million to $39 million and non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA to be approximately breakeven.

Our focus is now on translating these launches into sustained gains in customer acquisition, retention, and profitable growth.

Third Quarter Financial Highlights

 

  ∎  

Revenue In Line with Guidance – Third quarter revenue of $33.2 million was within our guidance range of $32.0 million to $35.0 million and declined 10% from $37.0 million in the third quarter of 2025. The year-over-year decline reflects the expected decline in Institutional revenue following our decision to wind down the VT4S business line, as well as lower Consumer revenue driven by fewer Active Members and lower ARPM.

 

  ∎  

Consumer Revenue – Consumer revenue was $30.7 million in the third quarter, down 7% year-over-year, and representing 93% of total revenue. As of September 30, 2026, Active Members totaled 31.8 thousand, down 7% year-over-year and ARPM was $366, down 2% year-over-year. Retention continued to improve year-over-year, but new client acquisition was lower. Several product enhancements and customer acquisition initiatives, including our self-service registration and checkout experience, are running more than three months behind our original plan, which weighed on third quarter acquisition. Some of these features have shipped, and the remainder will ship later in the fourth quarter. We expect them to support new client acquisition as they roll out.

 

  ∎  

Gross Margin – Gross margin on a GAAP basis was 59.7% for the third quarter. Excluding a $1.6 million charge for the abandonment of capitalized internal-use software, non-GAAP gross margin was 64.5%, an improvement of approximately 160 basis points compared to 62.9% in the third quarter of 2025. The improvement was primarily driven by lower amortization of capitalized internal-use software, reflecting abandonment charges recorded in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the third quarter of 2026. This impact was partially offset by higher Expert costs as sessions per member increased.

 

  ∎  

Net Loss and Adjusted EBITDA Improve Year-over-Year; Adjusted EBITDA Ahead of Guidance – Net loss was $12.8 million in the third quarter, compared to $18.7 million in the third quarter of 2025. Non-GAAP adjusted net loss, which excludes non-cash stock-based compensation, restructuring costs, and the charge for the abandonment of capitalized internal-use software, was $7.0 million, compared to $11.9 million in the prior-year period. Non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA loss was $5.7 million, ahead of our guidance range of negative $9.0 million to negative $6.0 million, and an improvement from a loss of $10.2 million in the third quarter of 2025. Performance relative to guidance was driven by lower sales staffing costs, lower AI costs, and disciplined G&A management. We also made meaningful progress on the elevated AI costs we noted last quarter: our annualized AI cost run-rate declined approximately 60% from its peak in June and July to September, driven by stronger governance and model optimization, without reducing employee adoption of AI. Year-over-year, AI-enabled efficiency improvements and continued cost discipline contributed to an improvement of more than 1,000 basis points in non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA margin.

 

  ∎  

Operating Cash Flow and Non-GAAP Free Cash Flow – Operating cash flow for the third quarter was negative $4.6 million, compared to negative $2.4 million in the same period in 2025. Non-GAAP free cash flow was negative $5.9 million, compared to negative $4.1 million in the same period in 2025. As we discussed last quarter, VT4S contracts were generally paid in advance, so exiting ahead of its peak booking season would reduce our cash collections this year, and that played out as we expected. Two items related to the wind-down drove the year-over-year change: the absence of approximately $5.1 million of prepaid VT4S bookings collected in the prior-year quarter, and $1.2 million of exit-related payments. Excluding these items, our free cash flow improved by approximately $4.5 million year-over-year.

 

  ∎  

Liquidity and Capital Resources – On September 28th, we borrowed the remaining $10.0 million available under the first tranche of our term loan, bringing total borrowings to $30.0 million. As of September 30, 2026, cash and cash equivalents were $42.5 million. We believe our cash on hand provides ample liquidity to fund operations and growth initiatives as we execute toward positive free cash flow.

Fourth Quarter 2026 Outlook

 

 

Today, we are introducing fourth quarter 2026 guidance.

 

The guidance reflects lower new client acquisition in the third quarter, which carries into a smaller Active Member base and lower Consumer revenue in the fourth quarter. Continued cost discipline offsets part of the revenue impact on non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA.

 

Our fourth quarter outlook reflects the timing of the product enhancements and customer acquisition initiatives. Some of these features are now live, and the remainder will ship later in the quarter. As they roll out, we expect the year-over-year gap in new client acquisition to narrow through the fourth quarter.

 

Revenue Guidance

 

●

For the fourth quarter of 2026, we expect revenue in the range of $37.0 million to $39.0 million.

 

Adjusted EBITDA Guidance

 

●

For the fourth quarter of 2026, we expect non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA to be approximately breakeven.

   

We expect to end the year with $35.0 million to $37.0 million of cash, inclusive of the $30.0 million drawn on our term loan. Based on our current operating plan, we expect existing liquidity to fund the company through free-cash-flow breakeven.

Financial Discussion

Revenue

Revenue for the three months ended September 30, 2026, was $33.2 million, a decrease of 10% from $37.0 million during the same period in 2025. Revenue for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, was $125.2 million, a decrease of 4% from $129.9 million during the same period in 2025.

Revenue for both the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, decreased when compared to the prior year period due to lower Consumer and Institutional revenue. Institutional revenue decreased in both current year periods as a result of our decision to wind down the VT4S business line. Consumer revenue for the three months ended September 30, 2026, decreased due to lower Active Member count and lower ARPM. Consumer revenue for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, decreased due to a lower Active Member count, partially offset by higher ARPM. While both current year periods were impacted by a lower Active Member count when compared to the corresponding prior year periods, we expect certain product enhancements and customer acquisition initiatives that we plan to release later in the fourth quarter to support new client acquisition.

Gross Profit and Gross Margin

Gross profit on a GAAP basis of $19.8 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, decreased by $3.5 million or 15% compared to the same period in 2025. Gross profit on a GAAP basis of $80.1 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, increased by $1.3 million or 2% compared to the same period in 2025. Gross margin on a GAAP basis was 59.7% and 62.9% for the three months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025, respectively. Gross margin was 64.0% and 60.6% for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025, respectively.

Cost of revenue for both current year periods was impacted by the wind-down of VT4S. This included a charge for the abandonment of capitalized internal-use software of $1.6 million in connection with the wind-down. Excluding the abandonment charge, non-GAAP gross profit of $21.4 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, decreased by $1.9 million or 8% compared to the same period in 2025. Excluding the abandonment charge, non-GAAP gross profit of $81.7 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, increased by $2.9 million or 4% compared to the same period in 2025. Excluding the abandonment charge, non-GAAP gross margin was 64.5% and 62.9% for the three months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025, respectively. Excluding the abandonment charge, non-GAAP gross margin was 65.3% and 60.6% for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025, respectively.

Cost of revenue included amortization expense related to capitalized internal-use software of $0.6 million and $1.7 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025, respectively, and $1.7 million and $5.1 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025, respectively. Excluding the impact of the abandonment charge, cost of revenue and gross margin improvements for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, were primarily driven by lower amortization of capitalized internal-use software as a result of charges for the abandonment of capitalized internal-use software that occurred in the third quarter of 2026 and the fourth quarter of 2025. These impacts were partially offset by higher Expert costs as sessions per member increased.

General and Administrative

General and administrative expenses include compensation for certain employees, support services, product and development expenses intended to support innovation, and other operating expenses. Net Loss, Non-GAAP Adjusted Net Loss, and Non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA (Loss)

Non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA loss was $5.7 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, ahead of our guidance range of negative $9.0 million to negative $6.0 million, and compared to a non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA loss of $10.2 million in the same period in 2025. Non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA loss was $5.6 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, compared to a non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA loss of $19.3 million in the same period in 2025.

Non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA performance relative to guidance was driven by lower sales staffing costs, lower AI costs, and disciplined G&A management. Key Operating Metrics

We monitor the following key operating metrics, among others, to evaluate the performance of our business.

Active Members is defined as the number of Learners with an active paid Learning Membership as of the date presented. Variations in the number of Active Members are due to changes in demand for our solutions, seasonality, testing schedules, and the launch of new membership options. As a result, we believe Active Members is a key indicator of our ability to attract, engage, and retain Learners. Active Members excludes our Institutional business. While our Active Member count as of September 30, 2026, was lower when compared to September 30, 2025, we expect certain product enhancements and customer acquisition initiatives that we plan to release later in the fourth quarter to support new client acquisition.

ARPM is defined as the average Consumer Learning Membership subscription revenue per member per month as of the date presented. Variations in ARPM are primarily due to changes in the mix of Learning Memberships sold and pricing changes. ARPM excludes our Institutional business. ARPM as of September 30, 2026, decreased compared to September 30, 2025, primarily due to the lapping of price increases enacted in February 2025 and a mix shift to lower frequency Learning Memberships. ARPM was higher than the prior year as of March 31, 2026 and June 30, 2026, reflecting the effect of those price increases.

Active Experts is defined as the number of Experts who have instructed one or more sessions in a given period. We believe Active Experts is a key indicator of our ability to service Learners and provide Experts with revenue-generating opportunities. Active Experts includes our Institutional business. Our Active Expert count during the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, decreased when compared to the prior year periods. This decrease was primarily due to lower Consumer Active Experts, driven by utilization of the highest quality Experts by encouraging them to work with more Learners and develop deeper relationships that allow for increased revenue-generating opportunities. Our Active Expert count during the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, was also impacted by the wind-down of VT4S. We believe our Active Expert count at September 30, 2026, is sufficient to meet our near-term growth objectives.

Key Performance Metrics and Non-GAAP Financial Measures

This earnings release includes non-GAAP financial measures for non-GAAP cost of revenue, non-GAAP gross profit, non-GAAP gross margin, non-GAAP sales and marketing expenses, non-GAAP general and administrative expenses, non-GAAP adjusted net earnings (loss), non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA (loss), and non-GAAP free cash flow.

Non-GAAP cost of revenue, gross profit, and gross margin exclude charges for the abandonment of capitalized internal-use software. Non-GAAP sales and marketing expenses exclude non-cash stock compensation expenses and restructuring costs. Non-GAAP general and administrative expenses exclude non-cash stock compensation expenses and restructuring costs.

Non-GAAP adjusted net earnings (loss) is defined as net income or net loss, as applicable, excluding non-cash stock-based compensation expenses, restructuring costs, and charges for the abandonment of capitalized internal-use software.

Non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA (loss) is defined as net income or net loss, as applicable, before net interest income (expense), taxes, depreciation and amortization expense, non-cash stock-based compensation expenses, restructuring costs, and charges for the abandonment of capitalized internal-use software.

Non-GAAP free cash flow is defined as net cash provided by (used in) operating activities less capital expenditures.

Sales and marketing expenses consist of salaries and benefits for our employees engaged in our consultative sales process. General and administrative expenses are recorded in the period in which they are incurred and include salaries, benefits, and non-cash stock-based compensation expense for certain employees as well as support services, product development, finance, legal, human resources, other administrative employees, information technology expenses, outside services, legal and accounting services, depreciation expense, and other costs required to support our operations.

Net loss per share is computed by dividing net loss by the weighted average number of shares outstanding during the period as calculated using the treasury stock and “if-converted” methods, as applicable.

Non-GAAP measures are in addition, and not a substitute for or superior to measures of financial performance prepared in accordance with GAAP and should not be considered as an alternative to sales, net income, operating income, cash flows from operations, or any other performance measures derived in accordance with GAAP. Other companies may calculate these non-GAAP financial measures differently, and therefore such financial measures may not be directly comparable to similarly titled measures of other companies. The Company believes that these non-GAAP measures of financial results provide useful supplemental information. The Company’s management uses these non-GAAP measures to evaluate the Company’s operating performance, trends, and to compare it against the performance of other companies. There are, however, a number of limitations related to the use of these non-GAAP measures and their nearest GAAP equivalents.
